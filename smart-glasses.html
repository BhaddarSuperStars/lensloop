Best Smart Glasses with Camera 2026 - Eydology AI Smart Glasses Review vs Meta Ray-Ban, Prescription Smart Glasses

Eydology's smart glasses (roughly $95–$159) add a camera, voice assistant, and Bluetooth calling to a regular-looking frame. Here's what that means day to day, and who they actually suit.

What they actually do

Three core features show up across the line: a built-in camera for hands-free photos and short video, a voice assistant for basic queries, and Bluetooth for phone calls and audio without earbuds. Some models are also available with a prescription lens (marked "optical") or a photochromic lens that darkens outdoors.

Who they're worth it for

Best fit if you already wear glasses daily and want to cut down on carrying a separate pair of earbuds, or you want a low-effort way to record point-of-view video (workouts, walks, hands-on tasks). Less compelling if you don't currently wear glasses — you'd be adding a frame for the tech alone.

Battery

Expect a single-day cycle

Camera and Bluetooth features draw noticeably more power than a plain frame. Treat these like another device that needs a nightly charge, not a set-and-forget pair of glasses.

Lens options

Optical vs. photochromic vs. standard

The "optical" variants take a prescription lens like a normal pair of glasses. Photochromic versions darken in sunlight, functionally doubling as sunglasses. If you want one pair to cover indoor and outdoor use, the photochromic option is the one to look at.

Privacy

Recording courtesy

A camera in a frame that looks like ordinary glasses means people around you often don't know it's recording. Worth a quick check of local recording-consent norms before using them in shared spaces.
